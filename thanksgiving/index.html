Every good gift is from above 各樣美善的恩賜
PLYMOUTH · AUTUMN 1621

「各樣美善的恩賜和各樣全備的賞賜，都是從上頭來的。」“Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above.”雅各書 1:17James 1:17

1621 普利茅斯的秋光與感恩Autumn Light & Gratitude Since 1621

一張安靜的卡片，送給你A quiet card, for you

在一個安靜的秋日，一張長桌擺在普利茅斯的海灣邊。清教徒與萬帕諾亞格人圍坐在一起，為著剛剛收穫的玉米、南瓜與野禽，低頭謝恩。桌上的食物不多，卻足夠讓一群歷經嚴冬、幾乎失去一切的人，重新學會一件事：恩典不是理所當然的。On a quiet autumn day, a long table stood by the bay at Plymouth. Pilgrims and Wampanoag sat together around it, bowing their heads in thanks for the corn, the pumpkins, and the wild fowl they had just harvested. The food was not plentiful, yet it was enough to teach a people who had survived a terrible winter, and lost nearly everything, one thing again: grace is never to be taken for granted.

這張卡片所記念的，正是那一刻——恩典從上頭來。雅各書說：「各樣美善的恩賜和各樣全備的賞賜都是從上頭來的。」就像嗎哪一樣，食物、友誼與平安，都不是我們掙來的，而是從天上緩緩降下的禮物。This card remembers that moment — the moment grace came down from above. As James writes, “Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above.” Like manna, food, friendship, and peace are not things we earn; they are gifts that descend gently from heaven.

歷史註記：1621 年秋天，普利茅斯殖民地與萬帕諾亞格人共進秋收筵席。愛德華·溫斯洛（Edward Winslow）在《莫特關係》（Mourt's Relation，1622）中記述了這場宴會；威廉·布拉德福德（William Bradford）在《普利茅斯種植園史》（Of Plymouth Plantation）中留下了殖民地早期的完整記載。文獻提到的食物包括鹿肉、野禽、印第安玉米、南瓜、豆類、魚、莓果與堅果。火雞是後來的傳統象徵，並非文獻的中心。A note on the history: in the autumn of 1621, the Plymouth colonists shared a harvest feast with the Wampanoag. Edward Winslow described the feast in Mourt's Relation (1622); William Bradford preserved the colony's early history in Of Plymouth Plantation. The foods named in the records include venison, wild fowl, Indian corn, pumpkins, beans, fish, berries, and nuts. The turkey is a later traditional symbol, not the centre of the record.

桌邊的人物The People at the Table

布魯斯特長老Elder William Brewster

約 1566–1644 · 治理長老c. 1566–1644 · Ruling Elder

清教徒的治理長老與屬靈父親，在第一個冬天為垂死者禱告。據傳統，他在 1621 年秋收筵席上領眾謝恩——正是畫中桌首低頭禱告的那一位。The Pilgrims' ruling elder and spiritual father, who prayed with the dying through the first winter. Tradition holds that he led the prayer of thanks at the 1621 harvest feast — the bowed head at the centre of the painting.

威廉·布拉德福德William Bradford

約 1590–1657 · 第二任總督c. 1590–1657 · Second Governor

普利茅斯殖民地總督三十年，《普利茅斯種植園史》（1630 年起筆）的作者——正是這部史書保存了清教徒的故事。1621 年秋收筵席上，他就是那位總督，五隻鹿正是獻給他的。Governor of Plymouth Colony for some thirty years, and author of Of Plymouth Plantation — the history, begun in 1630, that preserves the Pilgrims' story. He was the governor at the 1621 harvest feast, to whom the five deer were brought.

畫像皆為後人想像，並非寫生——布魯斯特與布拉德福德生前皆無畫像存世。Both likenesses are artists' imaginings, not portraits from life — no portrait of either man from life survives.

感恩詩歌Thanksgiving Poems

秋光灑在普利茅斯，
海灣靜靜聽禱聲。
玉米南瓜擺滿桌，
鹿肉魚鮮謝主恩。
嚴冬已過春又來，
存留性命非偶然。
各樣美善從上頭，
嗎哪降下似甘霖。
清教徒與原住民，
圍桌同坐一家親。
不同言語同一心，
感恩化作歌聲新。
願這恩典臨我家，
親人朋友同蒙福。
數算主恩心安息，
年年秋天唱感恩。

Autumn light on Plymouth Bay,
The tide keeps time with prayer.
Corn and pumpkin fill the board,
Venison and fish are there.
The winter passed, the spring returned,
Our lives were not our own.
Every good gift from above,
Like manna gently sown.
The Pilgrim and the Native son
Sit down as one at the board.
Different tongues, one thankful heart,
Our gratitude to the Lord.
May this grace fall on my home,
On family, friend, and call.
Counting mercies, hearts at rest,
Thanksgiving crowns it all.
